# Upstream port status

This is a working inventory, not a claim of complete parity. Life-Simulator1 and OpenLife are MIT-licensed Python console projects; Little Life adapts them to a static browser application. The upstream notices and revisions are recorded in `third_party/README.md`. Robert1811's repository has no verified reuse license and is not a source for copied code or content.

## Adapted so far

- Life-Simulator1 names and country catalogue (without regional rules); traits and trait learning; karma; school, university performance, scholarships, loans and tuition help; individual outings with enjoyment and refusal, relationship conversations with agreement and arguments, potential dates and compatibility, compliments, insults, gifts, requests for money, sibling attributes, conversation effects and possible retaliation, parent generosity and wealth ratings and repeated-request cooldowns, partner lifespans, sudden deaths of known-age relatives, kidnapping-loss funerals, queued funeral decisions and relationship duration, child deaths and memorials, child bonds, recorded co-parent succession and independent child profiles with simplified education, student loans, jobs, happiness and career records, and partner profiles with lifespans and recorded marriage dates, relationship interest and marriage durability; pregnancy decisions, simplified partner readiness and conception chance, and adoption agency offers; proposal cooldown and confirmation before cancelling an engagement, wedding venues, divorce settlements and bond-dependent vow renewal; age-specific toy and craft stories, creative blocks and reduced enjoyment with in-game depression; music, library, page-by-page books, movies, pets and outings; lottery; plastic surgery; work hours, stress, raise requests, confirmation before quitting or retiring, optional progressive tax and career pensions; multiple illness types, doctor/alternative treatment, and cancer or heart-attack death causes; surrender; selected event dialogue including dating invitations, lightning and kidnapping; recorded lifetime-happiness score.
- OpenLife degree and job names and interview questions and named promotion chains; diet options and additional conditions; life statistics; recorded death causes and family archive details; bank savings, debt and bankruptcy; investment market names and categories with single- and multi-unit trades; vehicle, boat, plane and home catalogue, licenses and vehicle depreciation; last wills and estate allocation; two books and outing dialogue.
- Browser-specific equivalents include up to ten local save slots, validated JSON backup, a timeline, a three-moment-per-year limit, and playable family generations. Several rules and amounts are intentionally rebalanced or simplified.

## Audited upstream placeholders

- In the inspected OpenLife `openlife.py` revision, the savings, loan, property, investments and crime menus contain entries without connected callbacks. Instruments, martial arts, walk and many activity-menu entries are also placeholders. The browser features under these headings are independent rebalanced implementations, not ports of executable upstream menu actions.

## Life-Simulator1 static data audit

At the revision in `third_party/README.md`, `python3 scripts/audit_lifesim.py /path/to/Life-Simulator1` finds 337 distinct top-level uppercase names in `const.py`; 18 are referenced by name elsewhere under `src` and 319 are not. The referenced gameplay lists include books, conversations, compliments, insults, music artists, family outings, sudden-death reasons and tax brackets, plus names and configuration. This is a static name scan, **not** proof of runtime reachability or a substitute for checking dynamic lookups, and it does not cover OpenLife. Many large lists for social media, jobs and sports appear in the constants file but are not referenced by name in the inspected `src` tree; they should not automatically be represented as executable gameplay.

The Life-Simulator1 country file contains names, while a search of its `src` tree found no use of that country list beyond an import of the package and no country-specific currency or tax implementation. The browser uses the names as locations and applies the same financial rules everywhere; recheck the other upstream project independently before making a broader parity claim.

## Remaining before claiming a full port

- Audit all executable upstream branches and all dialogue/data lists against the browser version, including the remaining relationship interactions and outcomes, life events and character details. Selected source dialogue is present, not every source line or template.
- Reconcile the upstream rules with the simplified browser systems: event selection currently shares a single annual story pool, death and happiness effects are rebalanced, high-school graduation is at 18 rather than 17, and parent requests use a three-refusal cooldown rather than reproducing upstream’s stuck refusal state. Also audit country-specific rules and currencies if implemented by the other upstream project, richer NPC attributes and histories, university and career progression, salary/tax/pension calculations, and full estate and family behavior.
- Finish and test any upstream systems that are only partially represented. Some OpenLife menu entries are placeholders with no connected implementation; distinguish those from working source features rather than calling them completed ports.
- Add migration, backup, accessibility and regression coverage for each remaining system. Chromium desktop and mobile smoke tests covered a new life, six years, modal keyboard dismissal, local slot, JSON backup export/import, reload, a child death with persisted funeral and memorial, and repeated parent requests; full end-to-end browser coverage remains outstanding. Do not merge a PR described as a full port until this inventory has been reconciled.
